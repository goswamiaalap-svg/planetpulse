# Reducing Car Travel Carbon Emissions

Personal vehicle travel is often one of the largest single contributors to household greenhouse gas emissions. According to the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), a typical passenger vehicle emits roughly 4.6 metric tons of carbon dioxide per year, averaging approximately 200 grams of CO2 per kilometer driven (0.20 kg/km).

## Key Strategies to Lower Driving Footprint

1. **Trip Chaining and Errand Consolidation**: Combining multiple short errands into a single trip significantly reduces cold-engine starts, which produce substantially higher emissions per kilometer than warmed engines.
2. **Carpooling and Ride Sharing**: Sharing a daily commute with one colleague cuts per-passenger commuting emissions by 50% immediately.
3. **Eco-Driving Habits**: Smooth acceleration, observing highway speed limits, maintaining recommended tire pressures, and minimizing excessive idling can improve fuel economy by 10% to 25%.
4. **Modal Shifts for Short Distances**: Over 50% of urban car trips are under 5 kilometers. Shifting these short trips to walking, bicycling, or micro-mobility eliminates tailpipe emissions completely.
5. **Vehicle Efficiency**: Transitioning to hybrid or battery-electric vehicles powered by cleaner grid energy offers lifecycle emission reductions of 50% to 70% compared to conventional internal combustion vehicles.
