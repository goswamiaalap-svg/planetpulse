# High-Speed Rail vs. Short-Haul Commercial Flights

For journeys between 200 km and 800 km (125 to 500 miles), modal shift from regional commercial aviation to electrified passenger rail yields dramatic carbon reductions.

## Quantitative Comparison per Passenger-Kilometer

1. **Aviation Factor**: Commercial flights average 0.25 kg CO2 per passenger-km, with additional high-altitude radiative forcing multiplying climate warming impacts during climb-out and cruising.
2. **Electric Passenger Rail**: Modern electric intercity passenger trains emit between 0.025 and 0.040 kg CO2 per passenger-kilometer (85% to 90% less carbon than flying). On renewable-powered high-speed lines, emissions drop to under 0.010 kg CO2/p-km.
3. **Corridor Substitution Example**: On a 500 km intercity route (e.g. London-Edinburgh or Paris-Lyon), flying generates 125 kg CO2 per passenger, while taking high-speed rail generates under 15 kg CO2—saving 110 kg CO2 per round trip.

## Sustainable Travel Decision Framework

- **Train Over Plane for Sub-4-Hour Routes**: Choose high-speed rail for trips where train travel time is 4 hours or less, eliminating airport security delays and city-center transfer transit emissions.
- **Night Trains for Long-Distance**: Utilize overnight sleeper trains for distances of 800–1,500 km as a practical, zero-hotel, low-carbon alternative to domestic flights.
