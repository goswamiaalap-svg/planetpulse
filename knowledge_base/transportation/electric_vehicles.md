# Electric Vehicles (EVs) vs. Internal Combustion Engine (ICE)

Transitioning personal mobility from conventional petroleum internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles to Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs) is a primary pathway for deep transportation decarbonization.

## Lifecycle Carbon Footprint Comparison

1. **Operating Emissions**: A standard gasoline passenger vehicle emits approximately 0.20 kg CO2 per kilometer (tailpipe). A typical electric vehicle consumes 0.15 to 0.20 kWh per kilometer. Under average grid electricity (0.80 kg CO2/kWh), an EV emits 0.12 to 0.16 kg CO2/km (a 20–40% reduction). On clean or renewable-rich grids (<0.20 kg CO2/kWh), EV operational emissions drop below 0.04 kg CO2/km (an 80% reduction).
2. **Manufacturing & Battery Payback**: Due to battery mineral extraction and cell manufacturing, building a BEV produces 30% to 50% more embedded emissions upfront than an ICE car. However, due to superior electric motor efficiency (90% vs 25% for ICE), the emissions "break-even" payback occurs within 15,000 to 25,000 km (1 to 2 years of typical driving).
3. **Plug-in Hybrids (PHEVs)**: PHEVs offer 40–80 km of pure electric range, cutting urban commute emissions significantly if charged daily, but carry the weight penalty and emissions of dual powertrains on long trips.

## Recommended Driving & Charging Practices

- **Smart Off-Peak Charging**: Charge during overnight off-peak hours or midday solar peak to access lowest-carbon electricity tariffs.
- **Regenerative Braking**: Utilize maximum regenerative braking modes (one-pedal driving) in urban traffic to recapture 15% to 25% of kinetic stopping energy back into the battery pack.
