# Dietary Carbon Footprint: Plant-Based vs. Meat Consumption

Food production contributes approximately 26% to 30% of total global greenhouse gas emissions. Agricultural emissions differ substantially between plant crops and livestock production.

## Emission Comparisons

1. **Emission Factors**:
   - Plant-based vegetarian meals produce on average ~0.5 kg of CO2 equivalent per standard serving.
   - Non-vegetarian meals containing red meat produce ~2.0 kg to 3.5 kg of CO2 equivalent per standard serving—approximately 4x to 7x higher impact.
2. **Methane and Ruminants**: Enteric fermentation in cows and sheep generates biogenic methane, a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential 28 times greater than CO2 over 100 years.
3. **Feed Conversion Inefficiencies**: Livestock require substantial land, feed crops, fertilizer, and freshwater. Feeding grain to animals instead of humans directly introduces multi-fold energy conversion losses.
4. **Actionable Substitutions**: Swapping 2 to 3 red-meat meals each week for legumes, tofu, lentils, or whole-grain vegetarian options can save up to 400 kg of CO2 equivalent per person annually.
