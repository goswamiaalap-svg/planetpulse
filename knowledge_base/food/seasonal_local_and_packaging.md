# Food Miles, Seasonality, and Packaging Emissions

While buying local food reduces transit mileage, rigorous life-cycle assessments (LCAs) demonstrate that transport typically accounts for less than 10% of food's total carbon footprint—what you eat matters far more than where it was grown.

## Carbon Breakdown Across the Food Supply Chain

1. **Production Dominance**: Agriculture, land-use change, and livestock farming generate over 80% to 85% of total food emissions. Transport accounts for only ~6% to 11%, and packaging contributes roughly 5%.
2. **The "Air-Freighted" Exception**: Most international food is transported via ocean container ships, which have very low emissions per kilogram (bulk efficiency). However, highly perishable items transported by air cargo (e.g. out-of-season berries, asparagus, or fresh cut flowers flown across continents) have emissions up to 50 times higher than sea-freighted goods.
3. **Heated Greenhouses**: Consuming local vegetables grown in fossil-fuel heated greenhouses in winter often generates higher emissions than importing field-grown seasonal vegetables from warmer climates.

## Decarbonization Priorities for Grocery Shopping

- **Prioritize Plant Protein Over Local Meat**: Eating a plant-based meal from imported grains and legumes produces far lower emissions than eating locally sourced beef or lamb.
- **Choose Seasonal, Field-Grown Produce**: Opt for seasonal fruits and vegetables to avoid both air-freight and artificial greenhouse heating emissions.
- **Bulk Refill & Reusable Packaging**: Reduce single-use plastic and aluminum container production energy by utilizing bulk dry-goods dispensers.
