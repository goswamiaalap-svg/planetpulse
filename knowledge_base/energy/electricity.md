# Home Energy and Electricity Consumption

Household electricity usage is driven by heating, ventilation, air conditioning (HVAC), water heating, refrigeration, and consumer electronics. The emissions generated depend heavily on the carbon intensity of the local electrical grid.

## Benchmark Emission Factors

1. **Grid Factor**: In fossil-fuel heavy or thermal grid environments, grid electricity averages roughly 0.80 kg CO2 per kilowatt-hour (kWh). In cleaner, hydro or nuclear-heavy grids, this drops below 0.20 kg/kWh.
2. **High-Draw Appliances**:
   - Electric space heating and central air conditioning typically account for 40% to 50% of total household electricity consumption.
   - Electric water heaters consume ~18% of residential power.
3. **High-Impact Reduction Actions**:
   - **Thermostat Optimization**: Setting the thermostat 1°C cooler in winter or 1°C warmer in summer cuts HVAC consumption by 5% to 8%.
   - **Vampire / Standby Load Reduction**: Using smart power strips to cut parasitic standby power on media centers and desktop computers saves up to 50–100 kWh annually.
   - **Heat Pump Technology**: Switching from resistive electric heating to high-efficiency air-source heat pumps cuts heating electricity use by over 50%.
   - **LED Lighting**: Replacing incandescent or halogen fixtures with LEDs reduces lighting wattage demand by 75% to 85%.
