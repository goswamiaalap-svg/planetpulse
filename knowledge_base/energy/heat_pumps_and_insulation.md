# Heat Pumps and Residential Thermal Efficiency

Space heating and water heating account for approximately 50% to 65% of total residential energy consumption globally. Replacing fossil fuel boilers and resistive electric baseboards with modern electric heat pumps is among the most effective domestic decarbonization actions available.

## Key Benchmark Data & Efficiency

1. **Coefficient of Performance (COP)**: Air-source heat pumps typically operate with a seasonal COP of 3.0 to 4.5, meaning they deliver 3 to 4.5 units of heat output for every 1 unit of electrical input (300% to 450% efficiency).
2. **Lifecycle Emissions Reduction**: When powered by standard municipal grid electricity (0.80 kg CO2/kWh), a heat pump reduces space heating greenhouse gas emissions by 50% compared to natural gas furnaces and 70% compared to oil boilers. On cleaner grids, emissions drop by over 80%.
3. **Home Weatherization Synergy**: Combining heat pump installation with basic attic insulation, window sealing, and double glazing reduces thermal leakage by 20% to 35%, allowing smaller heat pump sizing and reducing peak winter electricity demand.

## Recommended Household Actions

- **Upgrade to Cold-Climate Inverters**: Modern variable-speed inverters maintain high efficiency even in sub-zero winter temperatures down to -25°C (-13°F).
- **Smart Temperature Setbacks**: Maintain steady target temperatures (e.g. 19–20°C / 66–68°F) rather than wide nighttime drops to maximize heat pump seasonal efficiency.
