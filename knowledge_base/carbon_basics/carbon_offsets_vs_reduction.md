# Carbon Offsets vs. Direct Emissions Reductions

While voluntary carbon offsets allow individuals to fund climate projects (such as reforestation or clean cookstoves), international climate science establishes a strict mitigation hierarchy: direct emissions reduction must always take precedence over purchasing offsets.

## The Mitigation Hierarchy

1. **Direct Reduction First (Priority 1)**: Eliminating emissions at the source—such as replacing car trips with transit or switching to plant-based meals—permanently prevents carbon dioxide and methane from entering the atmosphere.
2. **Energy Efficiency & Electrification (Priority 2)**: Improving the efficiency of essential energy use (insulation, heat pumps, LED lighting) reduces the total clean energy capacity required.
3. **High-Integrity Carbon Offsetting (Priority 3 - Last Resort)**: Offsets should only be applied to counterbalance genuinely unavoidable residual emissions after all practical direct reductions have been achieved.

## Risks & Quality Criteria for Carbon Credits

- **Additionality**: An offset project must prove that the emissions reduction would not have occurred without the financial revenue from the carbon credit.
- **Permanence**: Nature-based offsets (e.g. forestry) are vulnerable to wildfires, droughts, and logging, releasing stored carbon back into the atmosphere within decades, whereas fossil fuel emissions persist for centuries.
- **Leakage**: Protecting a forest in one area must not simply displace logging to an adjacent unprotected parcel.
